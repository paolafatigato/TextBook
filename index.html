Summary

Matilda is an extraordinary little girl who loves books and is extremely intelligent. However, her parents do not appreciate her at all — they call her stupid and completely ignore her. At school, she meets a kind teacher called Miss Honey, who immediately recognises Matilda's genius. But the headmistress, Miss Trunchbull, is a horrible tyrant who terrorises children and teachers alike.

One day, Matilda discovers she has a magical power: she can move objects with her mind. She decides to use this extraordinary gift to help Miss Honey and to stand up to Miss Trunchbull once and for all.

Recommendation

I strongly recommend this book to anyone who has ever felt misunderstood or underestimated. It is perfect for readers aged 9–13 who love magical adventures and strong, independent characters. If you enjoyed the Harry Potter series, you will almost certainly love Matilda too.

Personal
comment

I really enjoyed this book because it shows that intelligence and kindness are more powerful than cruelty and ignorance. I particularly love how Matilda uses both her brain and her telekinesis to solve problems — she is a character you cannot help but admire. The story is funny and exciting, but it also has a deeper message: every child deserves love, encouragement, and support.

However, I think the descriptions of Matilda's parents are somewhat exaggerated — they seem almost too horrible to be believable. But perhaps that is precisely part of Roald Dahl's genius: by making the adults ridiculously bad, he makes Matilda's strength shine even brighter.

    The rules

    · Will 🔮 predictions, hopes & quick decisions

    ✅ I will (I'll) go❌ I will not (won't) go❓ Will you go?

    We use it for…Examples
    far future you can't do anything aboutThe sea turtles will go extinct.
    predictions without evidenceI think it will rain tomorrow.
    spontaneous decisionsYou look tired: I'll help you!
    hopes, wishes and requestsI hope you'll come. Will you help me?
    promises and threatsI won't tell anyone! · Stop or I'll scream!

    · Be going to 🎯 plans & intentions

    ✅ I am going to go❌ she isn't going to go❓ Are you going to go?

    We use it for…Examples
    plans and arrangements for the general futureI'm going to study medicine.
    intentions & resolutionsThis year I'm going to eat less sugar.
    predictions based on evidenceLook at those clouds! It's going to rain.

    · Present continuous 📅 things in your diary

    ✅ we are meeting❌ he isn't meeting❓ Are you meeting…?

    We use it for…Examples
    appointments (dentist…)I'm seeing the dentist at 4 tomorrow.
    fixed plans with specific times or dates (parties…)We're having a party on Saturday.
    programmed events (flights, weddings…)My cousin is getting married in June.
    verbs of movement, to avoid repetitionI'm going to the cinema (not going to go)

    · Present simple 🚆 timetables & schedules

    We use it for…Examples
    timetables (trains, buses, flights)The train leaves at 7.15 tomorrow.
    programmes and calendars (school, films, concerts)School starts on 14th September.

    💬 In spoken English going to often sounds like gonna: I'm gonna stop using plastic!
